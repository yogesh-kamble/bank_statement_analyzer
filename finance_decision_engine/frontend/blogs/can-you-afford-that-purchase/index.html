Can You Really Afford That Purchase? 5 Things to Check Before You Buy

An EMI that fits your monthly budget doesn't necessarily mean you can comfortably afford a purchase. Here's a simple framework to evaluate the impact of a major purchase on your savings, cash flow, and financial safety.

Financial Decision Engine • Purchase Planning

Buying something you want is easy. Deciding whether you can truly afford it is much harder.

A purchase can look affordable because the EMI fits comfortably within your monthly income. But affordability is about more than just the monthly payment.

A large purchase can reduce your savings, increase your monthly financial commitments, and reduce the amount of time your savings can support you if your income suddenly stops.

Before making an important purchase, it helps to look at the complete financial picture.

1. Start With Your Current Monthly Expenses

The first question is not: "Can I pay the EMI?"

A better question is: "How much money is already committed every month before this purchase?"

Start with your existing monthly expenses. These should represent what you already spend before adding the new purchase or EMI.

Example
Monthly Income ₹2,00,000
Current Expenses ₹1,00,000
New EMI ₹25,000
Post-Purchase Surplus ₹75,000

The new EMI is an additional commitment. It should therefore be evaluated against your existing monthly surplus.

2. Don't Ignore Your Savings

Monthly cash flow is only one part of financial safety.

A purchase can significantly reduce your savings even when the monthly EMI looks comfortable.

Consider a situation where you have ₹20 lakh in savings and want to make a ₹10 lakh purchase.

If you use an EMI and make a ₹2 lakh down payment, your immediate savings impact is ₹2 lakh rather than the full ₹10 lakh purchase amount.

Think about the money left after the purchase

The important question is not simply how much you had before buying. It is how much remains after the upfront payment and whether that amount still provides an adequate safety cushion.

3. Think About Your Emergency Runway

Your savings are not only there for planned purchases. They also provide protection when unexpected situations occur.

This is why it is useful to think about your emergency runway: how many months your remaining savings could support your financial needs.

Simple way to think about runway
Remaining Savings ÷ Monthly Financial Requirement
Example

Suppose you have ₹18 lakh remaining after your upfront purchase payment and your post-purchase monthly requirement is ₹1.25 lakh.

≈ 14 months

This is a simplified illustration. Your actual emergency requirement may vary depending on your financial situation.

A purchase that significantly reduces your emergency runway deserves more careful consideration, even if the monthly EMI is manageable.

4. Compare Cash and EMI Properly

There is no single payment method that is automatically better for every purchase.

Cash and EMI affect your finances in different ways.

💵

Pay With Cash

  • No new EMI
  • No loan interest
  • Larger immediate reduction in savings
  • No additional monthly payment obligation
💳

Pay With EMI

  • Smaller upfront savings impact
  • Adds a monthly obligation
  • Interest increases the total purchase cost
  • Requires sufficient monthly surplus

The right comparison is therefore not simply "cash versus EMI."

Instead, compare how each option affects your savings, monthly cash flow, emergency runway, and total financial commitment.

5. Look at Your Finances After the Purchase

One of the easiest mistakes is to evaluate the purchase using your financial position before buying it.

Instead, create a simple before-and-after picture.

Financial Metric Before Purchase After Purchase
Monthly Income ₹2,00,000 ₹2,00,000
Existing Expenses ₹1,00,000 ₹1,00,000
New EMI — ₹25,000
Monthly Surplus ₹1,00,000 ₹75,000
Savings ₹20,00,000 ₹18,00,000
Emergency Runway — ~14 months

This before-and-after view makes the financial impact of the purchase much easier to understand.

A Simple Pre-Purchase Checklist

Before making a significant purchase, ask yourself these questions:

✓
Can my current monthly cash flow support the purchase?

Look at your existing surplus before adding the new obligation.

✓
How much savings will remain?

Consider the upfront payment and other immediate costs.

✓
How long can my savings support me?

Consider your emergency runway after the purchase.

✓
What happens if my income stops?

A purchase should not leave your finances unnecessarily fragile.

✓
Have I compared cash and EMI?

Compare both the immediate and long-term financial impact.

Final Thought

Affordability is not simply about whether you can make the monthly payment.

A financially comfortable purchase should make sense not only today, but also after the purchase—when you look at your monthly cash flow, savings, emergency runway, and future commitments.

Taking a few minutes to understand these numbers before spending can help you make a much more informed financial decision.
